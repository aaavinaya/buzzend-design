## (b) Claim / wording rules (verbatim)

**Strategy §7 "What Buzzend Should NOT Claim"** (verbatim):
> Avoid claims that are broad, difficult to prove or potentially misleading.
> Do not claim:
> - "Perfect form"
> - "Injury-proof workouts"
> - "Replaces your personal trainer"
> - "Guaranteed results"
> - "Guaranteed weight loss"
> - "Medical-grade form analysis"
> - "Most accurate AI"
> - "World's first"
> - "Best fitness app"
> - "Scientifically proven" unless the exact claim is supported by appropriate evidence
>
> Preferred language:
> "Buzzend watches your movement and counts your reps."
> "Buzzend gives you workout guidance while you move."
> "Buzzend can provide form-related coaching cues."

**Strategy §40 "Special Rule for Rep Counting"** (verbatim):
> The rep-counting experience is the core product value.
> Marketing must never promise behavior that the actual engine does not reliably support.
> Before publishing a demo:
> - Test the exact exercise.
> - Test the exact camera position.
> - Test the exact device/platform.
> - Confirm the displayed count.
> - Confirm coaching behavior.
> - Avoid editing a video to imply functionality that does not exist.
> The marketing claim must match the real product.

**Strategy §6** (tech-lead ban): "Do not lead with technical implementation such as: MediaPipe, pose landmarks, classification algorithms, state machines, computer-vision architecture."

**Strategy §37 Words to use**: Move · Rep · Coach · Count · Challenge · Streak · Start · Progress · Home · Simple · Together · Try · Keep going. **Words to avoid**: Revolutionary · Perfect · Guaranteed · Medical-grade · Replace · Cure · Injury-proof · World's first.

**Strategy §21 Pillar 5**: "Do not expose sensitive implementation details or unsupported accuracy claims." **Pillar 1 (marketing OS)**: "We do NOT talk about: accuracy percentages, tech internals, competitor bashing, anything the app can't reliably do (§40)." **Pillar 2**: no "body transformations, weight loss promises, shame…, 'no excuses' grind culture."

**buzzend-marketing/00-strategy/what-is-buzzend.md**: "Never write or say: perfect form · guaranteed · injury-proof · replaces your trainer · medical-grade · most accurate · world's first · scientifically proven. Never lead with tech-speak: AI model, computer vision, pose landmarks, MediaPipe, algorithm." + "The one unbreakable content law: Never fake a rep count."

**README hard rules**: never publish `assets/screens/` (real account data); only `assets/screens-clean/` with approved demo identities (Ema William, Adesh Pokhrel, Kiran Shah, Riya, etc.); all assets local, no external URLs; "Marketing claims follow strategy §7 … and §40".

Other rules repeatedly enforced: exactly ONE CTA per piece; 3–5 hashtags; never paste raw store URLs in IG captions ("link in bio"); never ask strangers for ratings, no incentives, no star-steering (Day 13/32 review tasks); "Never market to children" (hard company rule). ## (c) Audience and market priorities

**Primary (§8, §53):** "The At-Home Starter" — age ≈18–35, beginner-to-intermediate, home/small space, no or minimal equipment, smartphone-heavy; wants consistency, strength, visible progress. Frustrations (use their words): "I lose count halfway through", "I don't know where to start", "Am I even doing this right?", "I always quit after a week", "I don't want anyone watching me at the gym", "Workout plans are too complicated". Install triggers: new goal, starting again, saw fitness content, friend challenge, home to save money/time.

**Secondary (§9):** Challenge Friend · Busy Professional · Returning Exerciser · Students/Young Adults ("do not automatically turn 'students' into 'children'").

**Not now (§10, §47):** advanced athletes, serious gym lifters, nutrition users, medical/rehab, trainers, schools, child-directed fitness, large institutions.
